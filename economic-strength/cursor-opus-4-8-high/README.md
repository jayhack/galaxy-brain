## What this is

A single, self-contained HTML data report — **"Two Economies, One Country"** — that makes an evidence-grounded case for how the US economy is performing and how ordinary Americans are experiencing it, **compiled June 26, 2026** with data through May–June 2026.

The thesis is deliberately two-sided and honest: by aggregate and international measures the US has the strongest large rich-world economy on the planet, while by household measures the typical American has run in place for five years and is being re-squeezed by a fresh inflation spike. The report walks the reader through that tension:

1. **The central paradox** — strong macro data vs. near-record-low consumer sentiment (the "vibecession").
2. **Why it feels bad** — the permanent ~29% jump in the price *level*, the latest energy-driven CPI re-acceleration (4.2%), real wages slipping again, and real median household income flat since 2019.
3. **The K-shaped economy** — the top 20% (and a record stock market) carrying spending while the bottom 80% trail inflation.
4. **International comparison** — US median disposable income 2nd in the world, yet paying ~double for healthcare and living 3.7 years less than peers.
5. **A bull-vs-bear verdict** and a **sourced figures table** so an evaluator can re-verify every headline number.

## How it renders offline

- One file, zero dependencies, no network calls.
- All figures are **hand-built inline SVG**, generated by a tiny vanilla-JS charting helper at load (bar, grouped-bar, and line charts). Nothing is fetched; it renders identically from `file://` on a fresh clone.

## Checking the work

Every headline statistic is attributed to a primary source (BLS, BEA, U.S. Census P60-286, NY Fed, OECD, University of Michigan, KFF/Peterson, Moody's Analytics) with its reference period, in the **Sources & headline figures** table at the bottom of the page. Because economic figures go stale, the compile date (June 26, 2026) is stated up top and the source links let an evaluator re-verify each claim.
